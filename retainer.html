The programme

One location. One film. Every two weeks.

The retainer exists to solve one problem: the map only means something if it keeps growing. A brand called Distant Local lives or dies on whether there is always somewhere new on it — and on whether each place gets treated properly rather than posted as a phone photo and forgotten.

So the unit of work isn't a shoot day. It's a pin: one location, fully covered, delivered as a complete package that drops onto the map and feeds two weeks of posting. The tiers below differ in one thing — how many pins land per month, and how far away they are.

What you're buying

  • A predictable cadence — a new location live every two weeks, planned a month ahead, not scrambled.
  • A growing library, organised and tagged, that keeps working long after the post.
  • Someone who already knows the brand, so no month starts from a blank page.

What this document doesn't cover

  • One-off campaign shoots — those are quoted separately, per concept.
  • Paid media management, posting, or copywriting.
  • Out-of-home, broadcast, or third-party licensing.
Anatomy of a pin

What one location delivers

Identical at every tier. A pin is a fixed package so the map stays visually consistent no matter whether the location is two hours down the coast or on the other side of the world.

How a pin gets chosen

  • It has to look like nowhere else on the map already — no two consecutive pins that read the same on a grid.
  • It has to be reachable without eating the shoot day: within the tier's radius, parked and walking by call time.
  • It has to have a reason — a season, a swell, a person who knows it, a thing you can only do there.
  • Permits, access, and private land cleared before it goes on the calendar, not the morning of.

How a pin gets shot

  • A pin is a half-day production block — roughly 5 hours on location, timed to one light window.
  • Where two pins sit within an hour of each other, they pair onto one full shoot day. That efficiency is exactly what pays for the Crossing discount.
  • Where they don't, they get two separate half days. Same deliverables either way.
  • Weather moves a pin to its backup date at no cost. A pin never gets shot badly to hit a calendar.

Cadence

What a Crossing month actually looks like

Two pins, offset by two weeks, each running the same four-step loop. While one is in the edit, the next is being scouted — so nothing waits on anything and a location goes live every fortnight. Shooting and publishing are deliberately decoupled: the two pins may well be shot on the same day.

Shoot days sit midweek by default — better light windows, emptier locations, and it leaves the weekend as a free weather backup. Each pin carries one named backup date from the moment it's scheduled.

The paired day

When both of a month's locations sit within about an hour of each other, they get shot on one full day — location one on the morning light, location two on the evening. Publishing stays two weeks apart; only the production consolidates.

This is not a corner cut. It's the single efficiency that makes $3,000 buy $4,400 of rate-card work, and it's why the shortlist gets built in geographic pairs rather than one location at a time.

When they can't be paired

  • Locations more than an hour apart, or on opposing tides or seasons, get two separate half days.
  • Same deliverables, same price — but it uses up the month's flexibility, so a second unpaired month in a row is a conversation about radius.
  • Where an overnight makes a distant pin possible, lodging is a pass-through at cost.
Meridian / on the road

Three or four pins, one trip, two ways to pay for it

Four locations in a month only works as a single trip — one flight in, one region, four stops, one flight home. Four separate journeys would cost more than the whole retainer. What changes is how far that region is, and that changes the money in a way worth being direct about.

Mode A — Short-haul · $6,500 all-in, four pins

One region reachable by a short flight or a long drive. Baja, the Pacific Northwest, the Rockies, Costa Rica — or Portugal and Morocco if we're staging from Europe.

  • Four locations plus the anthem film.
  • Travel included up to a $1,400 cap — flights, vehicle, fuel, lodging.
  • Talent stipends at each stop included, one person per pin.
  • 7–9 days on the ground.
  • Over the cap is billed at cost and approved first. Under the cap is credited back to you.

This is the cleanest trip month. One number, nothing to reconcile, and short-haul travel is small enough to sit inside the fee without squeezing the work.

Mode B — Long-haul · fee + travel at cost

Australia, France, Japan, Indonesia. Same deliverables, but the travel can't sit inside the fee — the flights alone are most of it.

  • $5,000 for three locations + anthem, or $6,500 for four. That's the shooting, editing, grading, and delivery.
  • Travel billed at cost against a cap we agree before anything is booked. Receipts, no markup.
  • Best case: Distant Local books the flights and lodging directly on your own card. You control the spend, there's nothing to reimburse, and it's a line you can see.
  • 10–14 days on the ground. Long-haul needs the extra days or the jet lag eats two pins.
  • Start at three. A first long-haul trip at $5,000 with three locations leaves room in the schedule for the things that go wrong in an unfamiliar country. Four is the second trip.

Why the fee and the travel are quoted separately. A single bundled number for a long-haul month would have to sit around $11,000–13,000, and most of it would be flights and hotels rather than work — which makes the invoice look far worse than the deal actually is, and leaves me carrying the currency and airfare risk. Splitting them keeps the pins at full quality, puts the travel spend on a line you control, and means a cheap flight is a saving for you rather than a margin for me. If you'd rather have one fixed number anyway, $11,000 all-in for a four-location long-haul month is the version I'll commit to, with anything under the travel cap credited back.

Booking a long-haul month

  • 60 days' notice minimum — flight prices and permit lead times both punish late decisions.
  • Region and rough pin list agreed before flights are booked.
  • Travel cap agreed in writing. Nothing booked above it without a yes.
  • Travel insurance and any visa costs are a pass-through line.
  • If the trip is cancelled after flights are booked, non-refundable costs stand; the creative fee is credited to the next month.

Sensible sequencing

  • Start with short-haul at $6,500. Prove the four-pin format works, see what a trip month does for the feed, then commit to Australia.
  • Run Crossing most months and drop in one Meridian month per quarter — that's the pattern I'd actually recommend. Steady local cadence, one big trip a season. Annualised, that's about $40,000 a year and roughly 30 locations on the map.
  • Two long-haul months a year is realistic. Four is a travel schedule, not a content programme.
