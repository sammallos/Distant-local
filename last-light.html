The read

Two decisions, priced separately

Coming out of our conversation there are two things on the table, and they should not be bundled into one number. Keeping them apart makes both easier to say yes to.

1 — The retainer

An ongoing monthly relationship: a predictable shoot cadence, a stocked content bank, and someone who already knows the brand so no month starts from zero. Priced monthly, three-month minimum.

2 — The fall shoot

A one-off campaign piece — bonfire, surf, cook, sunset — with its own concept, crew, and deliverables. Bookable standalone, or folded into the retainer at a discount.

Every figure here is a rate, not an estimate — no hidden line items appear later. Anything billed at cost (permits, lodging, mileage) is called out as such and approved in writing before it's spent.

How the retainer works

  • 3-month minimum, then month-to-month with 30 days' notice.
  • Invoiced on the 1st, in advance. Month one due at signing.
  • An unused shoot day rolls forward 30 days, then expires. Weather cancellations never expire.
  • Retainer clients get all à la carte work at 15% off the rate card.
  • Deliverable mix is flexible month to month — trade a video for extra stills, or bank two months into one bigger shoot, as long as it's agreed on the planning call.

Not included in any tier

  • Travel beyond 60 mi from base — mileage, lodging, and travel days billed per the crew section.
  • Talent fees and per diems beyond what a tier names.
  • Permits, location fees, studio rental — at cost.
  • Props, wardrobe, and food purchased for a shoot — at cost, receipts.
  • Out-of-home, broadcast, or third-party licensing — quoted per campaign.
  • Paid ad management, copywriting, or posting to your accounts.

05 / Direction

Cozy, but cold at the edges

The whole look lives on one tension: warm light against cold water. Firelight on a face, dark surf behind it. Wool over neoprene. Steam off a pan into wind. If a frame doesn't have both temperatures in it, it's not the shot.

Light

  • Sun under 15° for everything before the fire — long shadows, backlit spray, flare through a salt-dusted front element.
  • Once the sun's down, the fire is the only key. No LED panels pretending to be firelight. If it needs a lift, it's a bounce card or a hurricane lantern that's in frame anyway.
  • Underexpose the ambient a stop past sunset so faces read hot and the ocean goes deep blue-black.
  • Shoot into the dark until the fire is genuinely the brightest thing in the frame. That's the money hour.

Texture & wardrobe

  • Heavy knits pulled on over a half-peeled wetsuit. Beanies. A wool blanket over the shoulders.
  • Waxed canvas, cast iron, driftwood, enamel mugs, a battered cooler.
  • Bare feet in cold sand. Wet hair. Goosebumps. Nobody looks comfortable and that's the point.
  • Product is worn, not presented — sandy, damp, half-zipped, the way it looks on day two hundred.

Casting note

Real surfers and real friends, not models. People who can actually get a fire going and don't flinch at cold water. The camera should feel like a fourth person at the fire, not a crew of six pointed at two people. Nobody smiles at the lens.

Anti-references

No glossy studio product. No drone-heavy travel-influencer edit. No orange-and-teal grade pushed past believable. No music-video pacing — this is a slow, patient piece that earns its warmth.

06 / Treatment

Last Light — the film

Logline: A cold session ends the way the best ones do — a fire on the sand, a pan of something hot, and the last hour of light shared with people who stayed in the water too long.

No voiceover. No hard sell. The product is worn through every frame and never announced. The film's job is to make someone watching in November want to be cold and outside with their friends, wearing this.

The arc

ArriveTruck doors. Boards off the rack. Checking the water from the top of the dune. Almost no dialogue — just wind and the sound of gear.
Suit upZips, wax, the tailgate ritual. Detail frames where product does its actual work.
SessionGolden hour surf. Long lens from the sand, wide from the water line. Backlit spray. Not a highlight reel — the waiting, the paddling, the one good one.
Come in coldThe walk up the beach. Peeling a suit down to the waist. The first knit pulled on. This is the emotional pivot from cold to warm — it's the most important minute of the film.
Build the fireDriftwood, kindling, the first catch. Faces lit from below as the sun goes. Sun sets in this beat.
CookCast iron over coals. Something simple and good — fish, corn, bread, whatever's local. Steam into cold air. Knife on a board. Hands.
EatPlates on knees. Enamel mugs. Overheads of the food on a blanket. Real conversation, mostly unusable and entirely necessary for the sound bed.
EmbersFull dark. The fire's down to coals. Blankets. Long lens on faces. Last shot is the ocean going black behind the glow, then out.

07 / Logistics

What has to be true for this to work

Before we lock a date

  • Fire permit. Most coastline requires one, and many beaches only allow fires in designated rings. This gets confirmed before anything else — a no-fire beach kills the concept.
  • Tide. We want a low-to-mid outgoing tide at sunset so there's dry sand to work on and a wide beach in the wides.
  • Sunset time. Everything reverse-engineers from it. Mid-October gives a workable early-evening wrap; by mid-November we're wrapping in the cold and dark.
  • Burn ban status. Checked the week of, and again 24 hrs out.

Safety, non-negotiable

  • Water safety person on set for any in-surf work. Nobody shoots from the water alone.
  • Bucket, shovel, and water on site before the first match. Fire drowned and stirred at wrap.
  • Headlamps for every person for load-out.
  • Warm layers and hot drinks for talent between takes — cold, unhappy talent reads on camera immediately.
  • Leave-no-trace sweep. We are guests on that beach and I'd like to be able to shoot there again.

Weather

  • Go/no-go call is made 48 hrs out, together.
  • One named backup date held from the start. Moving to it costs nothing.
  • Overcast is fine — often better. Hard rain or a burn ban is the only real no-go.
  • If we've already spent on food, permits, or travel when we postpone, those costs carry to the new date rather than being re-billed.

Fallbacks

  • No fire allowed — camp stove and hurricane lanterns. Same warmth, smaller flame, still works.
  • Flat surf — the session becomes a paddle-out and a walk. The film's arc doesn't depend on a good wave.
  • Rain — move to the backup date. If we're already committed, we shoot the cook and the truck-bed version under a tarp and get a different, moodier film.
